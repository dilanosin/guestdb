The snakes you'll hear about

Six species Trinidadians know by name — and how to read them at a glance.

Fer-de-lance

"Mapepire"
Venomous — give space

The lancehead everyone warns you about. Shy, well-camouflaged pit viper; rarely aggressive unless startled.

Bushmaster

"Mapepire Z'Ancana"
Venomous — rare

The largest venomous snake in the Americas, found high in the Northern Range forests. Deeply reclusive.

Boa constrictor

"Macajuel"
Harmless constrictor

Trinidad's gentle giant. Non-venomous and a superb natural pest controller near homes and farms.

Coral snake

Small, banded
Venomous

Brilliant red, yellow and black bands. Non-aggressive and rarely encountered — admire, don't touch.

Tree boa

"Corde violet"
Harmless

A graceful arboreal boa often seen draped over forest branches. Excellent climber, feeds on birds & rodents.

Silky snake

"Cashew snake"
Harmless

A slim, gentle snake that loves cocoa and coffee estates. Eats soft-bodied prey and minds its business.

Where they call home

Two islands, one remarkable mix of habitats — and every one has its snakes.

  • ✓Northern Range — wet montane forest; look for boas, the bushmaster and tree boas.
  • ✓Cocoa & coffee estates — silky snakes and fer-de-lances hunting rats on the ridges.
  • ✓Lowland & savanna — macajuels and garden snakes around villages and fields.
  • ✓Tobago's forests — quieter, drier; a handful of the same familiar species.

Living beside snakes, safely

A few simple habits keep you, your family and the snakes safe.

Do not kill

All snakes are protected by the Conservation of Wildlife Act in T&T. Killing one is against the law.

Respect the Mapepire

Back away slowly and give it space. It will always move off on its own — attacks are almost always provoked.

Call for relocation

For a snake in your yard, contact the Wildlife Section or an experienced snake relocator — never handle one yourself.

If someone is bitten

Keep the person calm and still. Do not cut, suck or apply a tight tourniquet. Get to a hospital immediately.
